Why Salesforce

Three reasons this is the right platform for LSC

Drawn from what your team told us across four conversations

01 — One system, not a Frankenstein

The only option on your list that brings all of it into one platform

Your team was direct about why previous rollouts didn't take hold: a system stitched together from separate tools never became the place people actually worked. Adoption failed, and the data went back into inboxes and spreadsheets.

As you continue up your technology maturity curve, the question stops being which CRM is best and becomes which platform can still hold everything you're trying to streamline — business development, sales, proposals, project delivery, field operations, marketing and client experience. Salesforce is the only option under evaluation that covers all of it natively, on one data model, with one login. Nothing has to be bolted on beside it later.

02 — Off the shelf, and still shaped to your business

You don't have to choose between out of the box and a real fit

Most of what you asked for is standard: centralized client and contact management, pipeline tracking and forecasting, opportunity to proposal to project linkage, approval workflows, dashboards, Microsoft 365 integration, role-based permissions and auditability. No development, no custom build, no maintenance burden on your IT team.

Where your business is genuinely unique — tagging strategic opportunities by margin and relationship depth, flagging an opportunity with no contact in two weeks, scheduling technicians against certifications, your specific BD to account manager to proposal handoff — those are configured by an administrator, not coded by a developer. That's the difference between a packaged product that bends only as far as its assumptions allow, and a platform that fits the way you actually work.

03 — Capacity is the return

This is a revenue lever, not an efficiency cost

You're at capacity today. But that ceiling isn't entirely a headcount problem — a meaningful share of it is manual data entry, work re-created between systems, proposals chased through email, and project history reconstructed from memory.

Recover that time and you create real capacity. Combine it with deliberately pursuing repeat work from clients you've already earned, and getting your thought leadership in front of your top target accounts, and you don't just do the same work faster — you get to choose better work. Higher margin per hour, with the team you already have.

In practice

A day at Lake Superior Consulting

One client moving through your process — with the system carrying the record instead of people's memories

Business development

A conversation at a conference gets recorded

Your BD lead meets a reliability manager from a midstream operator at an industry event. Back in Outlook, the follow-up email is logged to a new lead in two clicks — source attributed to the event, so you can eventually tell which events actually produce work.

Business development to account management

Qualified and handed off cleanly

The lead converts into an account, contact and opportunity in one step. Before the account manager makes a call, the 360 view shows that a corrosion specialist already has a relationship at that company — so the outreach builds on it instead of duplicating it.

Sales and project controls

Proposal built where your team already works

Project controls drafts the proposal in the connected SharePoint folder, versioned as usual. Review tasks go to named people with due dates, and a formal approval step gates it before it reaches the client. The current draft is always findable from the opportunity.

Operations

Closed-won, with no re-entry

MuleSoft pushes the project to Unanet, which remains the system of record for hours, budget and billing. Work orders and a project record are generated in Salesforce automatically — no Power App re-entry, no new spreadsheet.

Project management

Scheduled against certifications, not guesswork

The dispatcher console shows which technicians hold the required certifications, who is available, and who is closest. Scheduling optimizes across all three. Utilization is visible — so when the next bid comes in, you know whether you actually have the capacity to take it.

Field operations

The crew works from a purpose-built app

Technicians see the work order, site details and required steps on a mobile app designed for field use. Notes, photos and completion status are captured on site, so the project record stays current without anyone writing it up later.

Client experience

Close-out actually happens

Project completion triggers the satisfaction capture your team wants — a structured close-out plus a survey link. The result lands on the account, giving you a measurable view of client health rather than an impression.

Marketing and BD

The project generates the next project

The account enters a nurture track. Your case studies, podcast and thought leadership go to them deliberately, and engagement scoring shows what they read. When they're ready for the next phase of work, you're already in the conversation — and the follow-on opportunity is in pipeline instead of lost.

Adoption

Your seller-doer culture means nearly everyone touches clients — which is why this needs 80 to 100 seats rather than a small team. A system that asks engineers and project managers to go somewhere new will not get used.

So capture happens inside Outlook and Teams, where the work already is, and field technicians get a purpose-built mobile app rather than a desktop CRM. Adoption is a design constraint here, not a training problem to solve after go-live.

Integration

Last time, the connection between systems had to be built and maintained by you. That is the part we want to take off your plate.

MuleSoft provides the connectivity layer to Unanet, PowerBI and SharePoint, and The Hunley Group will scope and price the integration before you commit — so it arrives as a defined deliverable with an owner and a number, not an open-ended project.
